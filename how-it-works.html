How It Works

No marketing framing here -- this is the actual methodology, with references to the real code that implements it. If a claim below isn't backtest-checkable or file-referenceable, we don't make it.

1 Where the data comes from

Every price, volume and fundamental figure comes from a real market data provider -- Alpaca first, yfinance as fallback when Alpaca doesn't cover a symbol -- the same pipeline every tool on this site shares (services/technical_analysis_service.py). Macro series (rates, inflation, liquidity) come from FRED's public API (services/fred_macro_service.py). Nothing is synthetic or hand-entered. When a data source isn't configured or a provider call fails, the affected feature honestly reports "available": false instead of silently falling back to a guess -- this pattern runs through the whole codebase, not just one endpoint.

2 The Confluence Engine

Every "score" and "direction" you see is a weighted vote across ~14 independent technical and macro signals -- trend, RSI, MACD, support/resistance proximity, Fibonacci, Bollinger position, OBV, SuperTrend, Donchian breakout, Ichimoku, Keltner, Gann levels, and (for Pro tiers) the Capital Flow Engine's regime read. Each signal votes bullish/bearish/neutral with a fixed weight reflecting how much that signal family has historically mattered relative to the others -- not tuned per-symbol to make any one result look better.

3 Backtests -- with real costs, not gross numbers

The homepage's Track Record section and every per-symbol backtest run the exact same live Confluence Engine strategy against real historical OHLC bars -- bar by bar, no look-ahead. Every indicator used is computed with backward-only rolling windows; the one signal that structurally needs future bars to confirm (fractal swing points) is deliberately excluded from backtests, even though it's used live, specifically to avoid a fake win rate.

Returns are reported net of a conservative cost assumption -- 0.05% commission + 0.05% slippage per side (0.2% round-trip) -- not the flattering gross number. The gross figure is still shown alongside, labeled, for anyone who wants to compare.

Source: services/backtest_service.py -- see DEFAULT_COMMISSION_PCT / DEFAULT_SLIPPAGE_PCT and the module's own docstring on look-ahead-bias avoidance.

4 ML models are gated, not trusted by default

Where a machine-learning model is used (e.g. the direction-probability forecast), it is never served unless it clears a statistical significance test first -- a one-sided binomial test comparing the model's out-of-sample holdout accuracy against a coin-flip baseline, at p < 0.05. A model that fails this gate returns "not available" for that symbol rather than a low-confidence number dressed up to look useful.

Source: services/direction_probability_service.py -- SIGNIFICANCE_ALPHA = 0.05, holdout accuracy vs. baseline, one-sided binomial p-value.

5 Forecasts are bands, never a fabricated single number

The Probabilistic Forecast (Bear/Base/Bull fan chart) is built by bootstrap-resampling real historical daily returns thousands of times and taking the 10th/50th/90th percentile of the simulated outcomes at each day of the horizon -- the same convention central banks use for GDP fan charts. We will never show you an invented "68% chance of a rally" figure that isn't the direct output of that simulation.

6 The Prediction Ledger -- graded against reality

Every direction-probability and Capital Flow forecast this site serves is automatically logged with the price at prediction time. Once each forecast's time horizon has passed, a scheduled job checks the real closing price and grades it -- correct or not, plus a Brier score for how well-calibrated the probability was. This isn't a self-reported backtest number; it's the same forecasts real users saw, checked against what the market actually did afterward.

Source: services/prediction_ledger_service.py -- auto-recorded per request, auto-graded on a daily schedule, hit rate and Brier score computed only over graded (already-resolved) predictions.

7 What we deliberately don't do

We don't cherry-pick the ticker basket a stat is computed over -- the homepage Track Record and Market Pulse widgets share the exact same fixed 8-asset basket (SPY/QQQ/DIA/IWM/XLK/XLF/XLE/BTC-USD), not a basket hand-picked because it backtests well.
We don't present a small sample as statistically meaningful -- every backtest result carries its trade count and an explicit caveat when the sample is thin.
We don't fake a feature that isn't ready -- unconfigured integrations (payment tiers, certain data feeds) honestly report unavailable rather than a placeholder that looks live.
We do label every AI-estimated or model-derived figure as such, distinct from figures pulled directly from a market data provider.

None of the above is investment advice. Backtests are historical simulations, not live trading records, and past performance doesn't guarantee future results.
